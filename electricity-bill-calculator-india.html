How is Your Electricity Bill Calculated?

A complete, simple guide to understanding electricity billing in India.

How Electricity Bill is Calculated in India

Your electricity bill in India is NOT simply Units × Rate. It is calculated in multiple layers:

  • Energy Charges: The core cost based on the number of units (kWh) consumed, calculated using tiered slab rates.
  • Fixed Charges: A fixed monthly amount charged regardless of how much you consume.
  • Fuel Adjustment Charge (FAC): A variable charge adjusted quarterly based on fuel costs of power generation.
  • Electricity Duty / Tax: State government tax, typically 5–20% on energy charges.
  • Meter Rent: A small monthly charge for the electricity meter provided by the DISCOM.
  • Late Payment Surcharge: Added if you pay after the due date — usually 1.5–2% per month.

Why Electricity Rates Differ State by State

India does not have a single national electricity rate. Each state has its own State Electricity Regulatory Commission (SERC) which sets tariff rates for that state based on the cost of power procurement, transmission losses, infrastructure, and government subsidies. This is why electricity in Kerala may be priced differently from UP, even though the national grid connects them.

Additionally, within a state, rates differ by consumer category — domestic households get subsidized rates, while commercial and industrial users pay higher rates to cross-subsidize domestic consumers.

The Slab System — Explained Simply

India uses a progressive slab system for electricity billing. This means you don't pay a single flat rate for all units — instead, the first few units are cheapest, and the rate increases as consumption rises. Think of it like income tax brackets, but for electricity.

Units ConsumedRate (per unit)Slab
0 – 100 units₹3.00/unitSlab 1 (Cheapest)
101 – 200 units₹4.50/unitSlab 2
201 – 400 units₹6.00/unitSlab 3
Above 400 units₹7.50/unitSlab 4 (Costliest)

So if you consume 250 units, you pay ₹3 for the first 100 units, ₹4.50 for the next 100 units, and ₹6 for the remaining 50 units — not ₹6 for all 250 units.

Real-Life Bill Examples

  • Small household (Uttar Pradesh, 150 units): ~₹520–₹600 including fixed charges and taxes
  • Medium household (Maharashtra, 300 units): ~₹1,400–₹1,700 with all charges
  • Heavy usage (Delhi, 600 units): ~₹3,200–₹3,800 after subsidy deduction for BPL category
  • Shop/Office (Tamil Nadu, 400 units, commercial): ~₹2,800–₹3,200 with commercial rates

Tips to Reduce Your Electricity Bill

  • Switch to 5-star BEE-rated appliances — especially ACs and refrigerators.
  • Replace all bulbs with LED lights — they use 70% less electricity than incandescent bulbs.
  • Set your AC to 24°C instead of 20°C — every degree rise saves ~6% energy.
  • Unplug appliances from standby — TVs, chargers, and set-top boxes consume power even when off.
  • Run washing machines and dishwashers on off-peak hours if your DISCOM offers ToD tariffs.
  • Install a rooftop solar panel — under PM Surya Ghar scheme you can get 300 free units/month.
  • Keep your refrigerator coils clean and ensure the door seal is tight.
  • Use natural light during the day and rely on fans instead of ACs in mild weather.

Why Tracking Your Electricity Usage Matters

Most Indians only look at their electricity bill when it arrives — by which time it's too late to reduce consumption for that month. By tracking usage weekly using our tools, you can spot unusually high consumption early, identify which appliance is consuming the most, plan when to run heavy appliances, and avoid bill shock at the end of the month.

❓ FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How is an electricity bill calculated in India?+
An electricity bill in India = Energy Charges (based on units × slab rate) + Fixed Charges + Fuel Adjustment Charge (FAC) + Electricity Duty/Tax + Meter Rent. The slab system means the first few units are billed at a lower rate and higher consumption falls into more expensive slabs.
Are electricity rates the same in all Indian states?+
No. Every state has its own State Electricity Regulatory Commission (SERC) that sets electricity tariffs. Rates vary significantly — for example, domestic tariffs in UP are lower than in Karnataka or Kerala. This is why state-wise calculators are essential.
What is the slab system in electricity billing?+
The slab system means your electricity is billed at different rates depending on how much you consume. For example: 0–100 units at ₹3/unit, 101–200 units at ₹4.5/unit, and so on. You only pay the higher rate on units that exceed the slab limit — not on your total consumption.
How can I reduce my electricity bill?+
Key ways to reduce your electricity bill: switch to LED lighting, use 5-star BEE-rated appliances, set your AC to 24°C, unplug standby devices, switch to a rooftop solar system, run washing machines during off-peak hours, and fix air leaks in air-conditioned rooms.
Is this electricity bill calculator accurate?+
Our tools use the latest published tariff rates from each state's electricity regulatory commission. They provide highly accurate estimates for domestic consumers. However, your exact bill may vary slightly due to fuel adjustment charges, local levies, or billing cycle differences. Always verify the final amount from your official DISCOM bill.
Can I use these tools for all Indian states?+
Yes! We have electricity bill calculators for all 28 Indian states and most Union Territories. Use the search bar above to quickly find your state. For states marked "Coming Soon", the tool will be live shortly.
How do I calculate units consumed from my meter?+
Units consumed = Current Meter Reading − Previous Meter Reading. For example, if your meter showed 5820 last month and shows 6150 this month, you consumed 330 units this billing cycle. Enter this value in our state calculator to get your bill estimate.
Are these electricity bill calculators free to use?+
Yes, 100% free — forever. No registration, no login, no subscription required. All calculations happen instantly in your browser. We don't collect or store any of your data. RajDailyTools is committed to building free tools for every Indian.
