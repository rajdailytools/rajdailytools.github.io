❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Australian salary and tax calculations.

How much tax do you pay in Australia? +
Australia uses a progressive tax system. For FY 2025–26, you pay 0% on income up to $18,200; 19% from $18,201–$45,000; 32.5% from $45,001–$120,000; 37% from $120,001–$180,000; and 45% above $180,000. A 2% Medicare levy also applies to most incomes. Most middle-income earners have an effective tax rate of 20–28%.
What is take-home salary in Australia? +
Take-home salary (also called net pay) is the amount deposited into your bank account after income tax and Medicare levy have been deducted. For example, on a $80,000 gross salary in FY 2025–26, your take-home is approximately $60,850 per year — roughly $5,071 per month.
What is superannuation and does it reduce my take-home pay? +
Superannuation (super) is Australia's compulsory retirement savings system. In FY 2025–26, your employer must contribute 11% of your salary into a super fund. Importantly, this is paid on top of your salary — it does NOT reduce your take-home pay. It's essentially free retirement savings your employer provides in addition to your wages.
How accurate is this Australia salary calculator? +
Our calculator uses the official ATO FY 2025–26 tax brackets, a 2% Medicare levy, and the Low Income Tax Offset (LITO) for eligible incomes. It provides accurate estimates for most Australian residents. It does not factor in salary sacrifice, the Medicare Levy Surcharge, private health rebates, work-related deductions, or foreign income. For complex situations, consult a registered tax agent.
Is this calculator updated for the 2025–2026 tax year? +
Yes. This calculator reflects the ATO's confirmed income tax rates and brackets for FY 2025–26 (1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026), including the 11% superannuation guarantee and the 2% Medicare levy. We update our tools each financial year to reflect any government changes.
Can I use this calculator for visa planning or migrating to Australia? +
Absolutely. This tool is popular among skilled migrants and people considering a move to Australia. It helps you understand your real take-home pay under the Australian tax system, which is essential for financial planning. Note that working holiday makers on 417/462 visas are taxed at a flat 15% on the first $45,000 — which is different from the resident tax rates used here. Verify your visa's tax residency status with the ATO.
