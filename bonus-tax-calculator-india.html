📖 Complete Guide

Everything About Bonus Tax in India

A comprehensive guide to understanding how bonus is taxed in India and how to save more.

💼 What is Bonus Tax in India?

In India, any bonus received from an employer — whether it's a performance bonus, annual bonus, festive bonus, or joining bonus — is treated as part of your salary income under the Income Tax Act, 1961. It is fully taxable in the year in which it is received, at the applicable income tax slab rate based on your total annual income.

There is no separate or lower tax rate for bonuses in India. The bonus simply gets added to your gross salary and the total is taxed according to the income tax slabs — Old Regime or New Regime — whichever you have opted for.

Key Point: Bonus is taxed at your marginal tax rate — the slab rate applicable to the topmost portion of your income. So if your income crosses ₹10 lakh after adding the bonus, that extra portion gets taxed at 30% (Old Regime).

🧮 How is Bonus Taxed? Step-by-Step

Here is how the tax on your bonus is calculated in India:

  • Step 1: Calculate your total annual income = Basic Salary + HRA + Allowances + Bonus + Other Income
  • Step 2: Subtract eligible deductions (80C, 80D, HRA exemption, etc.) if using Old Regime
  • Step 3: Apply the applicable tax slab rates to the taxable income
  • Step 4: Add 4% Health & Education Cess on the tax amount
  • Step 5: The difference between tax with bonus and tax without bonus = Tax attributable to bonus

📊 Old Regime vs New Regime — Which is Better?

Since 2020, India has two income tax regimes. The government made the New Regime the default regime from FY 2023–24. Here's how they compare:

Old Tax Regime allows multiple deductions (80C, HRA, 80D, LTA, NPS, etc.) but has higher base tax rates. It is beneficial if you have large investments, home loan, and can claim significant deductions.

New Tax Regime has lower tax rates across slabs but allows very few deductions (only standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried). It is beneficial if you do not have many investments or deductions to claim.

Rule of Thumb: If your total deductions (80C + HRA + 80D + NPS etc.) exceed ₹3.75 lakh, the Old Regime is likely better. Below that, the New Regime usually saves more tax. Use our "Compare Both" mode to find your exact answer.

📝 Real Examples — Tax on Bonus Calculation

Example 1 — Salary ₹5L + Bonus ₹1L (New Regime):

  • Total Income = ₹6,00,000
  • Standard Deduction = ₹75,000 → Taxable = ₹5,25,000
  • Tax: 0–3L = Nil, 3L–5.25L = 5% of ₹2.25L = ₹11,250
  • Cess (4%) = ₹450 → Total Tax = ₹11,700
  • Since taxable income ≤ ₹7L → 87A rebate applies → Tax = ₹0

Example 2 — Salary ₹10L + Bonus ₹2L (New Regime):

  • Total Income = ₹12,00,000 → After Std Deduction = ₹11,25,000
  • Tax = 0 + ₹20,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹22,500 + ₹18,750 = ₹91,250
  • Cess = ₹3,650 → Total Tax = ₹94,900
  • Tax on bonus portion (₹2L at marginal 15–20%) ≈ ₹28,000–₹32,000

❌ Common Mistakes People Make

  • Thinking bonus has a flat 30% tax — it depends on your total income slab
  • Not accounting for the 4% Health & Education Cess on top of tax
  • Forgetting that 87A rebate makes income up to ₹7L tax-free under New Regime
  • Using Old Regime calculators when New Regime is now the default
  • Not claiming all eligible deductions under Old Regime (HRA, 80C, 80D)
  • Assuming TDS deducted by employer is the exact tax — it may differ from actual liability

💡 Smart Tips to Save Tax on Bonus

  • Invest in 80C instruments (PPF, ELSS, LIC) up to ₹1.5 lakh under Old Regime
  • Claim HRA exemption if you are paying rent under Old Regime
  • Contribute to NPS for additional ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B)
  • Use 80D for health insurance premium — up to ₹25,000–₹50,000
  • Consider timing: if bonus comes in March, it adds to this year's income — plan accordingly
  • If your income is at a slab boundary, investing to reduce taxable income can move you to a lower slab
  • Compare both regimes every year as your salary and investments change

❓ Frequently Asked

Got Questions? We Have Answers.

Everything you need to know about bonus taxation in India.

💰 Is bonus taxable in India? +
Yes, bonus is fully taxable in India. It is treated as part of your salary income under Section 17(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. There is no exemption or separate lower rate for bonus income. The entire bonus amount gets added to your gross annual income and is taxed at the applicable slab rate for your total income.
🧮 How is bonus taxed — flat rate or slab rate? +
Bonus is taxed at your marginal slab rate — not a flat rate. Your employer adds the bonus to your salary and calculates TDS on the combined total. For example, if your salary puts you in the 20% slab, but after adding the bonus your income crosses ₹10 lakh (Old Regime), the bonus portion that crosses ₹10L will be taxed at 30%. This is why calculating slab-wise is important.
⚖️ Which tax regime is better for bonus income? +
It depends on your total income and the deductions you can claim. The New Regime is generally better if your total eligible deductions (80C, HRA, 80D, NPS, etc.) are below ₹3.75 lakh annually. If your deductions are higher — especially if you have a home loan, rent expenses, and max 80C investments — the Old Regime may save more tax. Use our "Compare Both" feature to get the exact comparison for your specific numbers.
💡 Can I save tax on my bonus amount? +
Yes, you can reduce your overall tax burden (which includes the bonus tax) by investing in 80C instruments (PPF, ELSS, LIC, NSC) up to ₹1.5 lakh under the Old Regime. Additionally, 80D for health insurance, 80CCD(1B) for NPS (₹50,000 extra), and HRA exemption can all help reduce your taxable income and thereby reduce the effective tax on your bonus. Note that these deductions are only available under the Old Regime.
✅ How accurate is this Bonus Tax Calculator? +
This calculator uses the official FY 2025–26 income tax slabs for both Old and New Regime as per the Income Tax Department of India. The mathematical formula for tax calculation is 100% accurate. However, it does not account for HRA exemption calculation, LTA, professional tax, or surcharge applicable on very high incomes (above ₹50L). For a comprehensive tax computation, consult a CA or use the official ITR portal.
👨‍💼 Should I consult a CA for bonus tax? +
For basic understanding and planning, this calculator is very useful. However, for complex situations — such as if you have income from multiple sources, capital gains, rental income, business income, or if you are in a high-income bracket with surcharge implications — consulting a Chartered Accountant (CA) is highly recommended. A CA can also help you optimize your salary structure to minimize tax on bonuses legally.
🏛️ What is the 87A rebate and does it apply to bonus income? +
Section 87A provides a tax rebate that makes your income tax nil if your total taxable income is up to ₹5 lakh (Old Regime) or ₹7 lakh (New Regime). This rebate applies to total income including bonus. So if after adding your bonus, your total taxable income stays under these limits, you pay zero tax. Our calculator automatically applies the 87A rebate where eligible.
