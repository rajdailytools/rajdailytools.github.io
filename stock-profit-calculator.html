Complete Guide

The Complete Guide to Stock Profit Calculation

📋 What is Stock Profit?

Stock profit is the financial gain you make when the selling price of a share is higher than the buying price. It is one of the two ways to earn from the stock market — the other being dividends. Understanding how to calculate stock profit accurately is the most fundamental skill for any investor, whether you are trading on NSE, BSE, or any global exchange.

The basic stock profit formula is deceptively simple: Profit = (Sell Price − Buy Price) × Quantity. However, the real-world calculation is more nuanced — brokerage fees, Securities Transaction Tax (STT), SEBI charges, GST on brokerage, and stamp duty all reduce your actual take-home profit.

Example: You buy 100 shares of Tata Motors at ₹850 and sell at ₹970. Your gross profit is ₹12,000. But after 0.5% brokerage (₹910) and 0.1% STT (₹97), your net profit is approximately ₹10,993 — nearly ₹1,000 less than the headline number.

📈 How Stock Profit Works in India (2026)

The Indian stock market — primarily NSE and BSE — saw significant activity in 2025 and early 2026, with Nifty 50 and Sensex reaching new highs. Understanding your real returns through a proper share profit calculator is more important than ever as more retail investors participate in the market.

When you execute a trade in India, the following charges apply:

  • Brokerage: 0.01%–0.5% depending on broker and trade type
  • STT (Securities Transaction Tax): 0.1% on delivery buy+sell, 0.025% on intraday sell side only
  • Exchange Transaction Charges: ~0.00345% (NSE) or ~0.00375% (BSE)
  • SEBI Turnover Fees: ₹10 per crore of turnover
  • GST: 18% on brokerage and exchange charges
  • Stamp Duty: 0.015% on buy side for delivery, 0.003% for intraday

⚡ Intraday vs Long-Term Stock Calculation

Intraday Trading

An intraday profit calculator must account for the fact that positions are opened and closed on the same trading day. Intraday STT in India is only 0.025% on the sell side (not both sides). However, intraday gains are taxed as business income — meaning they attract your income tax slab rate, not the 15% STCG rate. Risk management is critical as leverage amplifies both gains and losses.

Long-Term Investment

A long term stock calculator must factor in a different tax structure. For delivery trades held over 12 months, gains above ₹1 lakh per year attract Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax at 10% (as of April 2026). Held under 12 months, gains attract Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) at 15%. STT is 0.1% on both buy and sell for delivery trades.

Tax Note 2026: The Union Budget 2024 revised LTCG rates. Always verify current STT, LTCG, and STCG rates with SEBI or your broker as these are subject to change with each budget announcement.

🏦 How Brokerage Affects Your Actual Profit

This is the most underappreciated aspect of trading. With traditional brokers charging 0.3%–0.5%, brokerage alone can consume 30–50% of your profit on a small trade. Consider this real example from April 2026:

  • Buy 50 shares of Infosys at ₹1,800 (Investment: ₹90,000)
  • Sell at ₹1,920 (Gross Profit: ₹6,000)
  • Brokerage at 0.5% on both legs: ₹450 + ₹480 = ₹930
  • STT at 0.1%: ₹96
  • Net Profit: approximately ₹4,974 — 17% less than the gross figure

This is exactly why using a stock return calculator India that includes all charges is essential for accurate decision-making.

❌ Common Mistakes Investors Make

  • Calculating profit without deducting brokerage — overstating actual returns
  • Ignoring STT on delivery trades — 0.1% on large positions adds up significantly
  • Confusing gross profit % with net return % — always use net figures for decisions
  • Not accounting for GST on brokerage, which adds 18% to your brokerage cost
  • Applying long-term tax rates on short-term trades held less than 12 months
  • Forgetting to include stamp duty, which varies by state and instrument

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about stock profit calculation and this tool.

How to calculate stock profit?+
The basic stock profit formula is: Profit = (Sell Price − Buy Price) × Quantity. For the real net profit, you subtract brokerage charges (applied on buy + sell turnover) and STT/taxes (applied on sell value). Our calculator does all of this automatically. Simply enter your buy price, sell price, quantity, and optional brokerage/tax percentages, and the result appears instantly.
What is profit percentage in stock trading?+
Profit percentage = (Gross Profit ÷ Total Investment) × 100. For example, if you invested ₹1,00,000 and earned ₹12,000 in profit, your profit percentage is 12%. This figure helps you compare returns across different trades and benchmark them against Nifty, FD rates, or other investments. Our share profit calculator shows this figure automatically alongside absolute profit/loss.
Does brokerage really affect my profit that much?+
Yes — significantly, especially on smaller trades. If your gross profit is ₹2,000 on a trade and brokerage costs ₹600 (0.3% on ₹2,00,000 turnover), that's 30% of your profit gone. Flat-fee brokers like Zerodha (₹20/order) are more cost-efficient for larger trades, while percentage brokers are better for very small trades. Always use a stock profit calculator with brokerage factored in to see your true returns.
What is intraday profit and how is it different?+
Intraday profit comes from buying and selling a stock within the same trading session (same day). Key differences from delivery trading: STT is only 0.025% on the sell side (vs 0.1% on both sides for delivery), no stamp duty on buy, and gains are taxed as business income at your income tax slab rate (not STCG at 15%). Our intraday profit calculator works by entering the intraday STT rate (0.025%) in the tax field and adjusting brokerage accordingly.
Is this stock profit calculator accurate?+
The mathematical calculations are 100% accurate based on the inputs you provide. The tool applies the standard stock profit formula precisely. However, actual brokerage charges may differ slightly by broker as they also apply GST (18%) on brokerage, SEBI fees, and exchange transaction charges separately. For an exact figure, check your broker's contract note. This tool provides a very close estimate that is reliable for planning and decision-making purposes.
Can I use this for long-term investing calculations?+
Absolutely. As a long term stock calculator, this tool works perfectly. Enter your original buy price (even from years ago), your current or target sell price, and quantity. Use 0.1% in the tax field for STT on delivery trades. For LTCG (Long-Term Capital Gains) on holdings over 12 months, gains above ₹1 lakh are taxed at 10% — you can factor this into the tax field as well to see your post-tax returns.
How can I reduce losses in stock trading?+
The key strategies to reduce stock market losses are: (1) Always use a stop-loss order — decide your maximum acceptable loss before entering a trade. (2) Diversify across 8–15 stocks across different sectors. (3) Avoid trading with borrowed money or excessive leverage. (4) Choose low-cost brokers to minimize brokerage impact. (5) Focus on liquid, high-quality stocks. (6) Keep emotions out of decisions — use data and tools like this stock return calculator India to make objective assessments.
