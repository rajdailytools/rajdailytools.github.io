Complete Guide
Everything About Hourly & Annual Salary in USA

What is Hourly Salary in USA?

In the United States, many jobs — especially retail, hospitality, healthcare support, and entry-level tech roles — pay employees by the hour rather than a fixed annual salary. Your hourly wage is the amount you earn for each hour worked. The federal minimum wage is $7.25/hour, though many states have set higher minimums like $15/hour in California and New York.

To understand your full earning picture, you need to convert your hourly rate into weekly, monthly, and yearly figures. The standard formula in the US is: Annual Salary = Hourly Rate × 40 hours × 52 weeks = Hourly Rate × 2,080.

The magic number in US salary math is 2,080 — that's 40 hours/week × 52 weeks. So $1/hour = $2,080/year. A $20/hour job = $41,600/year. A $50/hour job = $104,000/year.

How Companies Calculate Salary in the USA

US employers calculate compensation in one of three ways depending on the role and contract type:

  • Hourly Pay: Common for part-time, retail, gig workers, and some full-time roles. Pay = Hours Worked × Hourly Rate. Eligible for overtime under FLSA.
  • Annual Salary: Common for office, tech, management, and professional roles. Paid in equal bi-weekly or semi-monthly installments regardless of hours worked.
  • Contract / Freelance Rate: Charged per hour, per project, or per milestone. No employer benefits — the rate must be higher to account for self-employment taxes and no paid leave.

Difference Between Hourly and Yearly Pay

The core difference goes beyond just the pay structure:

  • Overtime: Hourly workers are entitled to 1.5× their rate for hours over 40/week (FLSA). Salaried "exempt" employees typically are not.
  • Predictability: Salaried employees know their exact paycheck. Hourly workers' pay varies with hours worked.
  • Benefits: Full-time salaried employees often receive health insurance, 401k, PTO. Many hourly roles offer fewer benefits.
  • Flexibility: Hourly roles can offer more schedule flexibility. Salaried roles may demand unpaid extra hours.
Real Example: If you earn $25/hour and work 5 extra hours overtime per week, your weekly pay jumps from $1,000 to $1,187.50 (5 hrs × $37.50 OT rate). Over 52 weeks that's $9,750 extra — nearly 19% more income!

Freelancers vs Full-Time Income — What's the Real Difference?

Freelancers need to charge more per hour than salaried employees to earn the same take-home pay. Here's why:

  • Self-employment tax: Freelancers pay 15.3% SE tax on top of income tax. Salaried employees only pay the employee half (7.65%).
  • No paid leave: A salaried employee with 15 days PTO effectively earns salary even when not working. A freelancer earns nothing on holidays.
  • No employer benefits: Health insurance, 401k match, and other benefits can be worth $10,000–$20,000/year. Freelancers cover all this themselves.
  • Unpaid admin time: Freelancers spend hours on invoicing, marketing, and client management — none of which is billable.
Rule of Thumb: As a freelancer in the US, multiply your target salaried hourly equivalent by 1.5× to 2× to arrive at a fair freelance rate. If a job pays $40/hr as salary, charge $60–$80/hr as a freelancer to break even after taxes and benefits.

Common Mistakes People Make with Salary Calculations

These mistakes are surprisingly common — even among experienced professionals:

  • Forgetting taxes: A $50,000 salary doesn't mean $50,000 in hand. After federal, state, and FICA taxes, take-home is typically 65–75% of gross.
  • Ignoring overtime: Hourly workers who regularly work overtime can earn significantly more than the base calculation suggests. Always factor it in.
  • Not accounting for unpaid weeks: If you take 2 weeks unpaid leave, your actual annual income is based on 50 weeks, not 52.
  • Comparing hourly vs salary apples to oranges: A $60,000 salaried role with full benefits may be worth more than a $35/hour contract with no benefits.
  • Using wrong months: Monthly salary = Annual ÷ 12 (not ÷ 4.3 weeks). Many people multiply weekly pay by 4 and get a lower number.

Common Questions About Salary Conversion USA
How do I convert my hourly wage to yearly salary?+
Multiply your hourly rate by the number of hours you work per week, then by the number of weeks you work per year. The standard US formula is: Hourly Rate × 40 hours × 52 weeks = Annual Salary. Example: $20/hr × 40 × 52 = $41,600/year. Use our tool above to calculate any variation instantly.
What is the yearly salary for a 40-hour work week job?+
For a standard 40-hour week over 52 weeks (2,080 hours/year), the annual salary is simply your hourly rate × 2,080. Some quick examples: $15/hr = $31,200/yr; $20/hr = $41,600/yr; $25/hr = $52,000/yr; $30/hr = $62,400/yr; $50/hr = $104,000/yr.
How accurate is this salary calculator?+
The gross salary calculation (before tax) is 100% accurate using standard formulas. The tax estimation is an approximation based on the percentage you set using the slider. Actual tax depends on your filing status, deductions, state of residence, and other factors. Always consult a tax professional or use the IRS withholding estimator for exact figures.
Does this calculator include taxes?+
Yes — our tool includes an estimated tax deduction via the tax rate slider (0–45%). You can adjust it to match your estimated effective tax rate. The tool shows both gross income and estimated after-tax (net) take-home for all pay periods. Note this is an approximation, not a certified tax calculation.
What is the average salary in the USA?+
According to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, the median annual wage for full-time workers is approximately $59,000–$63,000 per year (around $28–$30/hour). Tech workers, doctors, lawyers, and engineers typically earn $80,000–$200,000+. Entry-level retail and service jobs typically range from $25,000–$40,000/year.
Can freelancers and self-employed workers use this tool?+
Absolutely. Freelancers can enter their hourly rate and adjust the hours/week and weeks/year fields to match their actual working schedule. Remember to set the tax slider higher (25–35%+) to account for self-employment tax on top of regular income tax. This helps estimate how much to set aside for quarterly tax payments.
How does overtime pay work in the USA?+
Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt hourly employees must be paid at least 1.5× their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek. For example, if you earn $20/hour and work 45 hours/week, the 5 overtime hours are paid at $30/hour (1.5× $20). Toggle the overtime option in our tool to include this in your calculation.
