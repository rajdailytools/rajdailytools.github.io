Indian Army Recruitment — Full 2026 Guide

Everything from the recruitment process to physical, medical and documentation standards, written clearly for first-time aspirants.

Complete Indian Army Recruitment Process 2026

Indian Army recruitment for soldier-level entries, including Agniveer, follows a structured multi-stage process. Understanding each stage in advance lets an aspirant prepare specifically instead of generally, which is what separates candidates who clear the rally from those who get eliminated on day one.

  • Notification & Online Registration: Recruitment notices are published on the Indian Army and Join Indian Army portals, specifying eligible districts, age limits, educational qualification, and rally dates.
  • Admit Card & Rally Reporting: Shortlisted candidates receive an admit card and must report to the designated rally ground with original documents on the specified date.
  • Physical Fitness Test (PFT): Running (1600m or 1 mile depending on category), long jump, and high jump are conducted first, since failing here typically ends participation for that day.
  • Physical Measurement Test (PMT): Height, weight, and chest (unexpanded and expanded) are measured by recruiting staff against category-specific standards.
  • Medical Examination: A detailed medical check covers vision, hearing, dental health, flat foot, knock knee, and general fitness, conducted by an Army medical officer.
  • Common Entrance Examination (CEE): Candidates who clear PFT, PMT, and medical rounds appear for a written exam, usually within a few weeks, covering General Knowledge, General Science, Mathematics, and Logical Reasoning depending on the entry category.
  • Merit List & Final Selection: A combined merit score from PFT, PMT (documentation verification), and CEE marks decides final selection, followed by a re-medical for shortlisted candidates in many cases.
Key Point: Clearing the physical rounds only gets you to the written exam — it does not guarantee selection. The CEE typically carries the largest weight in the final merit score, so physical preparation and exam preparation must run in parallel, not one after the other.

Indian Army Physical Standards Explained

Physical standards are checked on the rally ground itself and are non-negotiable on the day. They are broadly grouped into height, weight, and chest requirements, each varying by category, gender, and sometimes by region due to historical relaxations for hill and northeastern states.

Height Rules

General category male candidates typically need a minimum height of around 170cm, while several notified hill areas, northeastern states, and Gorkha regions carry relaxed minimums that can be as low as 162-163cm. Female candidates generally need around 152cm, with similar regional relaxations applying. Exact figures are published in each year's official notification and can shift slightly between recruitment cycles.

Weight Rules

Weight standards are proportionate to height and age rather than a single fixed number. A candidate's weight is checked against a standard height-weight-age chart during the PMT and again during the detailed medical exam, where BMI is also assessed for overall fitness suitability.

Chest Rules

Chest measurement has two components — the unexpanded (normal breathing) measurement and the expanded (full inhale) measurement. The difference between the two, known as chest expansion, must meet a minimum threshold, generally around 5cm, regardless of the base unexpanded size.

✅ Typically Acceptable

  • Unexpanded chest meeting minimum for height/category
  • Expansion of 5cm or more
  • Symmetrical, proportionate chest development

❌ Common Rejection Reasons

  • Expansion below the 5cm minimum
  • Underdeveloped unexpanded chest for stated height
  • Visible chest asymmetry flagged during measurement

Army Running Rules & Physical Fitness Test

The running test is usually the very first event at the rally ground and is often the biggest filter — a large share of candidates who report are eliminated here before height and chest are even measured. The distance depends on entry type: most General Duty and technical soldier entries run 1600m, while some categories use a 1 mile benchmark instead.

Faster running groups translate directly into higher PFT marks, which then feed into the final merit score alongside the written exam. This is why many aspirants who are otherwise academically strong still lose out on merit purely due to a slower running group — running speed is not just a qualifying hurdle, it is a scoring event.

Common Mistake: Aspirants often train only to "just clear" Group III instead of aiming for Group I or II, missing out on the extra merit marks that a faster time provides. Always train toward the highest group you can realistically reach, not just the qualifying minimum.

Army Medical Standards — Eye Test, Flat Foot & Knock Knee

The medical examination is detailed and covers general health, vision, hearing, dental status, and structural conditions of the feet and legs. Many candidates who pass the physical and running rounds are surprised to be stopped at this stage for conditions they were unaware they had.

Eye Test Standards

Most combat and general duty roles require 6/6 vision in one eye and at least 6/9 in the other without glasses or contact lenses, along with normal colour vision. Some technical and non-combat trades allow slightly relaxed vision standards with correction, but colour blindness is generally disqualifying for most roles regardless of trade.

Flat Foot

Flat foot is assessed by observing the foot's arch, typically using a wet footprint test or direct visual inspection while standing. Mild flat foot with a functional arch under load is sometimes passed at the discretion of the examining medical officer, while moderate to severe flat foot without any visible arch usually results in medical disqualification.

Knock Knee

Knock knee (genu valgum) is checked by having the candidate stand with feet together — if the knees touch while there remains a significant gap between the ankles beyond an acceptable range, it is flagged as a structural abnormality that can affect marching and running performance over a career.

Get Checked Early: Flat foot and knock knee are structural conditions that cannot be fixed quickly through exercise before a rally. If you suspect either condition, get assessed by a doctor months in advance so you know your actual standing before investing in months of running and physical preparation.

Documentation Checklist & Verification

Documentation is checked at multiple stages — an initial verification at the rally ground and a more detailed one before the written exam and final merit list. Missing, mismatched, or incorrectly filled documents are one of the most common and entirely avoidable reasons candidates are turned away.

  • Admit Card: Printed clearly, matching the name and photograph on your ID.
  • Educational Certificates: Original mark sheets and certificates plus photocopies for the qualifying exam specified in your entry category.
  • Domicile/Residence Certificate: Required to confirm you belong to the notified recruiting district.
  • Caste Certificate: Required only if applying under a reserved category quota.
  • Character Certificate: Usually issued by a Gram Panchayat, Sarpanch, or equivalent local authority, dated within the validity window specified in the notification.
  • Relationship Certificate: Required for certain quota entries such as sports or relationship-based categories.
  • Passport-size Photographs: Recent, matching the specification (usually a plain background, specific size) mentioned in the notification.

Common Mistakes Aspirants Make

  • Not verifying category-specific height/chest cutoffs before travelling to the rally, assuming the general standard applies everywhere.
  • Training only for a qualifying running time instead of aiming higher for better PFT marks.
  • Ignoring minor medical conditions like flat foot or vision issues until the rally day itself.
  • Carrying photocopies without originals, or an expired character certificate.
  • Filling the online application carelessly, leading to name, date of birth, or category mismatches flagged later as form mistakes.
  • Underestimating the written exam after clearing physical rounds, since CEE marks usually decide the final merit far more than PFT marks alone.

Preparation Tips & Ideal Daily Routine

A balanced preparation plan splits time between physical training, medical readiness, and written exam study. The routine below is a general template that many successful aspirants follow in the 3-6 months leading up to a rally.

5:00 AM
Wake up, light stretching, then a 20-30 minute warm-up jog before the main running session.
5:30 AM
1600m timed run practice 3-4 days a week, alternating with interval sprints and long-distance endurance runs on other days.
6:30 AM
Long jump and high jump practice, plus basic strength work (push-ups, pull-ups, squats) for overall PFT readiness.
8:00 AM
Breakfast with adequate protein, then rest and recovery before academic study begins.
10:00 AM – 1:00 PM
Written exam preparation — General Knowledge, General Science, Mathematics, and Reasoning as per your entry category syllabus.
5:00 PM
Evening light run or mobility work, avoiding overtraining before scheduled rest days.
9:30 PM
Early sleep to support recovery — 7-8 hours is standard advice among successful Agniveer aspirants.
Balance is Everything: Candidates who only train physically and neglect the written exam, or vice versa, consistently underperform on the final merit list. Use a fixed weekly schedule that protects time for both from the very start of your preparation window.

4 Pillars of Army Rally Preparation

Every successful aspirant builds preparation around these four pillars.

🏃
Physical Fitness
Build running speed progressively over months, not weeks. Combine interval training with long steady runs, and track your 1600m time weekly using a proper stopwatch, not estimates.
🩺
Medical Readiness
Get a basic health check months in advance — vision, dental, and foot structure especially — so there are no surprises on rally day that months of physical training cannot fix in time.
📄
Documentation
Prepare and photocopy every document weeks ahead, cross-checking spellings and dates against your admit card. A single mismatch can cause rejection regardless of your fitness level.
📚
Written Exam Prep
Dedicate fixed daily hours to General Knowledge, Mathematics, and Reasoning right from the start — CEE marks typically carry the largest share of your final merit score.

Frequently Asked Questions

20 common questions about Indian Army recruitment, physical standards, and preparation — answered clearly.

What is the minimum height required for Indian Army recruitment?+
General category male candidates typically need around 170cm in most states, while several hill and northeastern states carry relaxed minimums starting near 162cm. Female candidates generally need around 152cm. Always check the exact figure in your year's official notification.
How much chest expansion is required for Army recruitment?+
Candidates must show a minimum unexpanded chest suited to their height along with an expansion of at least 5cm between unexpanded and expanded measurement. Many successful candidates train toward 6-8cm expansion to stay comfortably clear of the cutoff.
What is the qualifying time for the 1600m run?+
Group I typically requires under 5:30, Group II under 5:45, and Group III under 6:30, though exact cutoffs can vary slightly by recruitment cycle, category, and entry type. Faster groups earn higher PFT marks.
What is flat foot and does it disqualify Army candidates?+
Flat foot is a condition where the foot's arch does not form properly. Moderate to severe cases without any visible arch typically lead to medical disqualification, while mild cases with a functional arch are sometimes passed at the medical officer's discretion.
What is the minimum eyesight requirement for Indian Army?+
Most combat and general duty roles require 6/6 vision in one eye and at least 6/9 in the other without correction, along with normal colour vision. Some technical trades allow limited correction, but requirements vary by role.
How is the Army merit list prepared?+
The final merit list combines PFT marks (based on your running group and other events), documentation verification through PMT, and marks from the written Common Entrance Examination, with CEE usually carrying the largest weight.
Can I apply for Army Rally if I fail chest expansion?+
No. Chest measurement is checked physically on the rally ground and candidates who do not meet the minimum unexpanded size and expansion are generally not permitted to proceed further on that day.
What documents are required for Army Rally?+
Common requirements include the admit card, original and photocopied educational certificates, domicile certificate, caste certificate if applicable, character certificate, relationship certificate for certain entries, and recent passport-size photographs.
Is there age relaxation for Agniveer recruitment?+
Certain categories have carried age relaxations in specific notifications. Candidates should always verify the exact relaxation applicable to their category in the current year's official notification rather than assuming a past year's rule still applies.
What is knock knee and how is it checked?+
Knock knee is a condition where the knees angle inward and touch while the ankles remain apart when standing. It is checked visually and with a simple standing test during the medical exam, and significant cases can lead to disqualification.
How many attempts can I make for Army Rally?+
There is generally no fixed limit on the number of attempts as long as you remain within the eligible age bracket for your entry category. Each notification cycle is treated as a fresh opportunity.
Do I need to clear running before chest and height are checked?+
Yes, in most rally formats the running event is conducted first, and only candidates who qualify proceed to height, chest, and weight measurement, followed by the detailed medical examination.
Can tattoos affect Army eligibility?+
Tattoo policy has evolved over recent years and generally restricts location and content of tattoos rather than banning them outright, though the specifics differ by notification. Always check the current tattoo policy in your rally notification before applying.
What happens after I clear the written exam?+
After clearing the CEE, shortlisted candidates typically undergo a detailed re-medical examination, and final selection is based on the combined merit of PFT, documentation, and CEE marks within the notified vacancies.
Is swimming or additional physical events required?+
Standard soldier-level rallies generally test running, long jump, and high jump. Additional events like swimming are specific to select entries or unit-level requirements rather than the general recruitment process.
How can I improve my running time before rally?+
Combine interval sprint training with steady long-distance runs across a structured multi-month plan, track your timed 1600m weekly, and allow adequate recovery days to avoid injury from overtraining close to your rally date.
Does dental health matter in the Army medical exam?+
Yes, dental health is checked as part of the general medical examination, and significant untreated dental issues can be flagged. Getting a basic dental check-up before your rally is a good precaution.
Are these tools free to use?+
Yes, all 21 tools in this hub are completely free with no login or signup required. They are designed to give aspirants an instant self-check before they travel for rally or written exams.
Can women aspirants use these tools?+
Yes, tools such as the height checker, BMI calculator, and eligibility checker support female-specific standards where applicable. Always verify female-specific cutoffs from the official notification for your target entry.
Do these calculators guarantee selection?+
No. These tools provide an estimate based on commonly published standards to help you self-assess and prepare. Final eligibility and selection are decided solely by the Indian Army through its official recruitment process.
