SSC Selection Post Phase XIV/2026 — Syllabus Overview

As per Para 15.9 of the official notification (Advertisement No. Phase-XIV/2026/Selection Posts), the Commission has published an "Indicative Syllabus for Computer Based Examination (CBE)" — given separately for three Educational Qualification levels, since the CBE itself is conducted as three distinct examinations (Para 15.1).

Each of the three syllabi (Para 15.9.1 for Matriculation, Para 15.9.2 for Higher Secondary/10+2, and Para 15.9.3 for Graduation & above) is structured identically around the same four subjects, but with topic depth and question style that increases with the qualification level:

  • General Intelligence (Part A) — moves from purely non-verbal reasoning at Matriculation level, to both verbal and non-verbal reasoning at Higher Secondary and Graduation levels.
  • General Awareness (Part B) — broadly similar scope across all three levels: environment, current affairs, and India & neighbouring countries (History, Culture, Geography, Polity, Economy, Science).
  • Quantitative Aptitude (Part C) — expands from basic arithmetic at Matriculation level, to include Algebra, Geometry, Mensuration, Trigonometry and Statistical Charts at Higher Secondary and Graduation levels.
  • English Language (Part D) — moves from basic grammar/vocabulary at Matriculation level, to detailed grammar-transformation topics at Higher Secondary level, and general comprehension/writing ability at Graduation level.

For posts requiring Graduation & above qualification, the notification specifies an important nuance: Parts A, B and D are set at Graduation level, while Part-C (Quantitative Aptitude) is kept at 10th standard (Para 15.9.3, final paragraph).

Important Topics & High-Weightage Topics

Information not specified in the official notification: The Commission does not assign extra marks or "high-weightage" status to any individual topic. Each Part (A, B, C, D) carries an equal 50 marks — 25 questions × 2 marks each — regardless of which topic within that part a question is drawn from (Para 15.1).

What the notification does make clear is which topics recur across all three levels for a given subject — these represent the core, consistently-tested areas as per the syllabus text itself:

  • General Intelligence: Number Series, Coding-Decoding, Classification and Analogy-type reasoning appear across the Higher Secondary and Graduation level syllabus.
  • General Awareness: History, Culture, Geography, General Polity, Economic Scene and Scientific Research are named identically at all 3 levels.
  • Quantitative Aptitude: Percentage, Ratio & Proportion, Averages, Profit & Loss, Time & Work / Time & Distance appear at all 3 levels; Algebra, Geometry, Mensuration and Trigonometry are added only from Higher Secondary level onward.
  • English Language: Vocabulary-based testing (Synonyms/Antonyms) is common to Matriculation and Higher Secondary levels; Graduation level shifts focus to comprehension and writing ability.
Preparation Strategy

A subject-by-subject approach mapped directly to the official Indicative Syllabus

1
Identify your exact CBE level first
Confirm from your target post's Educational Qualification whether you'll appear for the Matriculation, Higher Secondary (10+2) or Graduation & above level CBE — the syllabus depth differs meaningfully between levels, especially for Quantitative Aptitude.
2
Build General Intelligence topic-by-topic
Work through each named topic in your level's chip list (e.g. Coding-Decoding, Number Series, Venn Diagrams) individually, since the syllabus lists them as distinct testable areas rather than a single broad "reasoning" category.
3
Keep a running Current Affairs log
Since General Awareness explicitly covers "current affairs/events," maintain a running log of monthly current affairs alongside static History, Culture, Geography and Polity revision.
4
Sequence Quantitative Aptitude by difficulty
Start with the topics common to all levels (Percentage, Ratio, Averages, Time & Work), then move to Algebra/Geometry/Trigonometry only if your level requires them.
5
Practice English within the sectional timer
Each part has its own 15-minute sectional timer (Para 15.1) — practice English Language questions strictly within 15 minutes to build exam-day pacing.

Subject-wise Preparation Tips

Tips tied directly to what each subject's official syllabus actually tests

🧠
General Intelligence
Since Matriculation level is non-verbal only while Higher Secondary & Graduation add verbal reasoning, check your level first and don't over-prepare verbal topics if you're at Matriculation level.
🌍
General Awareness
The syllabus explicitly says questions "do not require special study of any discipline" — prioritise current affairs and everyday scientific observation over deep subject-specific study.
🔢
Quantitative Aptitude
If you're applying for a Graduation-level post, remember Part-C is set at 10th standard — don't over-invest time in advanced graduation-level maths.
📖
English Language
Higher Secondary level tests very specific grammar-transformation skills (Active/Passive, Narration) — practice these transformation types specifically, not just general vocabulary.
⏱️
Sectional Timer Discipline
Each part has its own 15-minute sectional timer (Para 15.1) — you cannot borrow time from one part for another, so practice each subject strictly within its own time limit.
➖
Negative Marking Awareness
With 0.50 negative marks per wrong answer, build subject-wise accuracy benchmarks in mock tests before attempting borderline questions in the real CBE.

Common Mistakes

Frequent syllabus-related errors candidates make

❌ Studying the wrong level's syllabus
Preparing the Graduation-level Quantitative Aptitude syllabus when your post is at Matriculation level (or vice-versa) wastes preparation time — always confirm your exact level first.
❌ Ignoring the sectional timer
Each part has its own 15-minute timer — practising without simulating this structure leads to poor time management on exam day.
❌ Over-preparing Part-C at Graduation level
Spending excess time on advanced-level maths when Part-C for Graduation-level posts is only of 10th standard, as stated in Para 15.9.3.
❌ Treating the syllabus as exhaustive
The notification itself titles this the "Indicative Syllabus" — treating the listed topics as a rigid, complete boundary can leave gaps in preparation.
❌ Skipping current affairs for General Awareness
The syllabus specifically names "current affairs/events" — relying only on static GK revision misses this explicitly-stated component.
❌ Not accounting for negative marking
Guessing every question without accuracy practice against the 0.50 negative marking rule can reduce your net score significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers based on the official SSC Selection Post Phase-XIV/2026 notification

SSC Selection Post Phase XIV/2026 me kitne subjects aate hain?+
4 subjects — General Intelligence, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude aur English Language. Har subject 25 questions aur 50 marks ka hota hai (Para 15.1).
Syllabus kitne levels ke liye alag hai?+
Teen levels — Matriculation, Higher Secondary (10+2) aur Graduation & above. Har level ka apna "Indicative Syllabus" hai (Para 15.9.1, 15.9.2, 15.9.3).
Graduation level ke liye Quantitative Aptitude kis standard ki hoti hai?+
Part-C (Quantitative Aptitude) 10th standard ki hoti hai, jabki Parts A, B aur D Graduation level ki hoti hain (Para 15.9.3).
Negative marking kitni hai?+
Har galat jawab ke liye 0.50 marks kaate jaate hain (Para 15.2).
Matriculation level ka General Intelligence syllabus kaisa hai?+
Sirf non-verbal type questions hote hain — similarities/differences, space visualization, problem solving, figure classification, arithmetical number series, non-verbal series, etc. (Para 15.9.1-A).
CBE ka total duration kitna hai?+
60 minutes total, har part ke liye 15 minute ka sectional timer. Scribe-eligible candidates ke liye 80 minutes (20 min/part) (Para 15.1).
Kya syllabus me topic-wise weightage bataya gaya hai?+
Nahi, notification me koi topic-wise ya subject-wise extra weightage nahi diya gaya — har part equal 50 marks ka hota hai.
"Indicative Syllabus" ka matlab kya hai?+
SSC khud is syllabus ko "Indicative Syllabus" kehta hai — yani ye topics representative hain, exhaustive/complete list nahi (Para 15.9).
CBE kis medium me hoti hai?+
Hindi aur English dono medium me hoti hai. Candidates ko application form me Sr. No. 23 par apna medium select karna hota hai (Para 15.4).
Previous year ka syllabus trend kahan milega?+
Information not specified in the official notification — Phase-XIV/2026 ek fresh notification hai jisme previous year trend data nahi diya gaya. Ye alag se ssc.gov.in par purane result/cut-off notices me dekha ja sakta hai.
