Complete IELTS Guide

Everything You Need to Know About IELTS

What is IELTS?

IELTS — the International English Language Testing System — is the world's most widely recognised English language proficiency test for non-native speakers. It is jointly owned and administered by the British Council, IDP: IELTS Australia, and Cambridge Assessment English, making it one of the most trusted language qualifications globally.

Each year, more than three million people sit the IELTS test across 140 countries at over 1,600 test centres worldwide. Universities, employers, immigration authorities, and professional licensing bodies in English-speaking countries accept IELTS scores as evidence of language ability.

Key fact: IELTS is accepted by over 11,000 organisations in more than 140 countries — including universities, employers, and immigration departments — making it the most globally recognised English test.

Types of IELTS Exams

There are two versions of the IELTS test, designed for different purposes. Both share the same Listening and Speaking tests, but differ in the Reading and Writing sections.

📘
IELTS Academic
For undergraduate or postgraduate university admissions and professional registration in nursing, medicine, or engineering. Uses complex academic texts.
📗
IELTS General Training
For secondary education, work experience, and migration to Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the UK. Uses practical everyday texts and situations.
🖥️
IELTS on Computer
The same test content as paper-based IELTS but taken on a computer. Results are returned faster — typically within 3–5 days instead of 13 days.
🏠
IELTS Indicator
An online practice test version available in some markets. Not accepted by all institutions — always check with your target university or visa authority first.

IELTS Scoring System Explained

IELTS uses a nine-band scoring system, running from Band 1 (Non-User) to Band 9 (Expert User). Scores are reported in whole or half-band increments — so you can achieve a 6.0, 6.5, 7.0, and so on. There is no passing or failing in IELTS; the score simply reflects your current level of English proficiency.

Each of the four skills — Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking — receives its own individual band score. These four scores are then averaged to produce your Overall Band Score, which is rounded to the nearest 0.5.

Rounding rule: If your four section scores average to 6.125, your overall band is 6.0. If they average to 6.375, your overall band is 6.5. The rounding is always to the nearest half-band.

How IELTS Band Scores Work in Practice

Understanding band scores is more than just knowing the number — it is about knowing what that number means for your goals. Here is a practical breakdown of what each band range typically unlocks:

  • Band 9.0: Expert-level. Virtually unrestricted use. Suitable for the most demanding academic programs and professional contexts.
  • Band 8.0 – 8.5: Very high proficiency. Meets requirements for competitive postgraduate programs, skilled migration, and professional licensing.
  • Band 7.0 – 7.5: Good operational English. Required for most top-tier universities and PR pathways in Australia, Canada, and the UK.
  • Band 6.5: The most common minimum for postgraduate entry worldwide. Also the benchmark for most work visas in English-speaking countries.
  • Band 6.0: Minimum for many undergraduate programs and some migration pathways. Often the gateway band for international students.
  • Band 5.0 – 5.5: Pre-sessional English or foundation course entry. Indicates a developing user who needs structured support before academic study.
  • Below Band 5.0: Requires significant English development before IELTS is a realistic short-term goal.

How to Prepare for IELTS Effectively

The most effective IELTS preparation is structured, consistent, and skill-specific. Broad general practice helps, but strategic preparation that targets your specific weak points is what produces meaningful band improvements, particularly when moving from band 6.0 to 7.0 or above.

🎧
Listening Strategy
Practice active listening with varied accents. Focus on prediction skills — reading ahead in the question paper while listening is one of the highest-value techniques.
📖
Reading Strategy
Master skimming and scanning before attempting detailed reading. T/F/NG questions are the most misunderstood type — practice these specifically to gain extra marks.
✍️
Writing Strategy
Plan before writing. Task 2 essays need a clear position, supported paragraphs, and a consistent argument. Most Band 5–6 writers lose marks on Task Response, not grammar.
🗣️
Speaking Strategy
Fluency beats accuracy at Band 6–7. Speaking with confidence and natural flow matters more than perfect grammar. Record yourself to identify hesitation patterns.

Common IELTS Mistakes to Avoid

These are the mistakes that consistently hold candidates back — knowing them in advance gives you a significant preparation advantage.

⏱️

Poor Time Management

Running out of time in Reading or Writing is one of the most common Band 6 problems. Practise under timed conditions from the very first mock test.

❓

Misunderstanding T/F/NG

"False" means the text contradicts the statement. "Not Given" means the text says nothing about it. Mixing these up loses three to four marks per test.

📝

Off-Topic Writing

Task 2 essays that go off-topic are penalised heavily under Task Response — the most heavily weighted criterion. Always answer the exact question asked.

🔤

Spelling Errors in Listening

Incorrect spelling in Listening answers is marked wrong even if the answer is correct. Practice spelling the words you hear, not just identifying them.

🗣️

Short Speaking Answers

One-word or one-sentence answers in Speaking Part 1 and 2 limit your score regardless of accuracy. Expand every answer with a reason or example.

📚

Ignoring the Exam Format

Sitting full practice tests only in the final week is not enough. Familiarity with question types and transitions between sections is built over weeks, not days.

University Admission and IELTS Requirements

Most English-medium universities require an IELTS score as part of their admissions process for international students. The minimum required band varies significantly depending on the institution's ranking, the program's language demands, and the country of study.

As a general guide, top-ranked universities in the UK, Australia, and Canada typically require an overall band of 6.5 to 7.0 for undergraduate programs and 7.0 to 7.5 for postgraduate programs. Some faculties — particularly medicine, law, and clinical psychology — have higher requirements than the general university minimum, often requiring each individual section to meet a specific band, not just the overall score.

Pro tip: Always check the section-specific requirements, not just the overall band requirement. Many nursing and medical programs require no individual section below Band 7.0, which is more demanding than just achieving 7.0 overall.

Scholarships and IELTS

Many of the world's most prestigious international scholarships require a minimum IELTS score as part of their eligibility criteria. The good news is that most scholarship IELTS requirements are achievable with focused preparation — and a strong IELTS score often signals language readiness that scholarship committees value beyond just the number.

  • Chevening Scholarships (UK): Minimum overall Band 6.5, with no component below 5.5 (check current guidelines annually).
  • Commonwealth Scholarships: No fixed IELTS requirement for some categories, but most awarding universities require 6.0–7.0.
  • DAAD Scholarships (Germany): German OR English language requirement; IELTS 6.0–7.0 accepted for English-medium programs.
  • Erasmus Mundus: IELTS requirements vary by consortium — typically 6.0 to 7.0 overall.
  • Government of Australia Awards: IELTS Academic overall 6.5 minimum.
  • New Zealand Aid Programme: IELTS 6.0 minimum, with no component below 5.5.

IELTS Preparation Strategy — How to Raise Your Band

Moving from Band 6.0 to 6.5 or from 6.5 to 7.0 requires a very different approach from a beginner starting from Band 5. At intermediate and upper-intermediate levels, the gains come from precision and consistency rather than exposure alone. Here is a skill-by-skill strategy framework for mid-level candidates:

For Listening (targeting 7.0+)

The single biggest gain at this level comes from improving your prediction skills — scanning the questions before each section to anticipate the answer type, format, and likely vocabulary. Most Band 6 Listening errors happen at the beginning of each section before the candidate has fully oriented themselves. Spend two minutes with the question paper before each section during practice, even if the exam does not allow this in some centres.

For Reading (targeting 7.0+)

At Band 6.5, most candidates understand the main ideas in each passage but lose marks on specific detail questions and Not Given answers. The key insight is that IELTS Reading tests close reading, not speed reading. Slowing down to read the specific paragraph in question carefully — rather than skimming and guessing — is frequently worth two to four extra marks.

For Writing (targeting 7.0+)

Task 2 at Band 7.0 requires a clearly stated position, logically sequenced paragraphs, and consistent use of cohesive devices without over-using them. The most common Band 6.5-to-7.0 blocker is overusing transition phrases such as "Firstly, Secondly, In conclusion" without genuine cohesion between ideas. Vary your linking mechanisms and develop each argument with a specific example or explanation.

For Speaking (targeting 7.0+)

The leap from Band 6.5 to 7.0 in Speaking is almost entirely about extending responses naturally. Band 7 candidates rarely hesitate, self-correct, or repeat themselves. They give full, connected answers in Part 1, a well-structured two-minute response in Part 2, and engaged, developed answers in Part 3. Practice speaking continuously on unfamiliar topics for two minutes — without notes — to build the fluency that examiners need to hear to award Band 7.

Frequently Asked Questions

IELTS FAQ — 28 Key Questions Answered

Everything you need to know about IELTS — from basic scoring to advanced preparation, university admission, scholarships, and visas.

What is IELTS and who administers it?
+
IELTS stands for International English Language Testing System. It is the world's most popular English proficiency test for higher education and global migration. It is jointly owned by the British Council, IDP: IELTS Australia, and Cambridge Assessment English. More than three million tests are taken every year in over 140 countries.
What is the difference between IELTS Academic and IELTS General Training?
+
Both versions share the same Listening and Speaking tests. The difference is in Reading and Writing. Academic IELTS uses complex texts from academic journals, books, and research publications. General Training IELTS uses everyday texts — notices, advertisements, workplace communications, and short informational passages. Academic IELTS is required for university admissions and professional registration. General Training is used for secondary education, work experience, and migration applications.
How is the IELTS Overall Band Score calculated?
+
The Overall Band Score is the average of your four individual section scores — Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking. The average is then rounded to the nearest 0.5 band. For example, if your four scores are 7.0, 6.5, 6.0, and 7.0, your average is 6.625, which rounds to 6.5 overall. You can use our IELTS Overall Band Score Calculator to check this instantly.
How long are IELTS scores valid?
+
IELTS scores are valid for exactly two years from the date of the test. After the two-year period, the score is no longer considered a current reflection of your English ability and most organisations will not accept it. If you need to use your IELTS score after two years, you will need to sit the exam again. Use our IELTS Score Validity Checker to confirm whether your score is still within the two-year window.
What is a good IELTS band score for university admission?
+
This depends on the university and program. As a general guide: most undergraduate programs require Band 6.0 to 6.5; most postgraduate programs require Band 6.5 to 7.0; top-ranked universities and competitive programs typically require 7.0 to 7.5 or above. Some programs also set minimum section scores, so always check the specific requirements for your target institution rather than relying on the general university minimum.
How many questions are in each IELTS section?
+
The IELTS test has 40 questions in Listening (four sections, 10 questions each) and 40 questions in Reading (three passages or sections). Writing has two tasks — Task 1 and Task 2 — and Speaking has three parts. Listening and Reading are objectively marked; Writing and Speaking are assessed by trained human examiners using four criteria each.
How is IELTS Listening scored?
+
In IELTS Listening, each correct answer is worth one mark, for a raw score out of 40. This raw score is then converted to an IELTS band on a scale of 1 to 9 using a standard conversion table. There are no marks deducted for wrong answers, so you should always write an answer even if you are not sure. The same conversion table is used for both Academic and General Training Listening because both versions take the same Listening test.
How is IELTS Reading scored?
+
IELTS Reading is also scored out of 40, with one mark per correct answer. However, Academic and General Training Reading use different conversion tables — so the same raw score can produce a slightly different band depending on which format you are taking. General Training Reading is generally considered slightly easier, which is reflected in the conversion table requiring more correct answers to achieve the same band. Use our IELTS Reading Band Score Calculator to check either format.
How is IELTS Writing scored?
+
IELTS Writing is assessed by trained examiners across four criteria: Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2), Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Each criterion is scored on a 1–9 band scale. For Writing Task 1, all four criteria are weighted equally. For Writing Task 2, all four criteria are equally weighted but Task 2 carries twice the marks of Task 1 in the overall Writing score calculation.
How is IELTS Speaking scored?
+
IELTS Speaking is assessed by a trained examiner in a face-to-face interview using four criteria: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation. All four criteria are equally weighted, and the examiner gives a band score for each before calculating the average. The Speaking test has three parts: a brief personal interview (Part 1), a one-minute preparation and two-minute long turn on a given topic (Part 2), and a two-way discussion on abstract themes related to Part 2 (Part 3).
How many times can I take IELTS?
+
There is no official limit to how many times you can take IELTS. You can sit the test as often as you like, though there is no minimum waiting period between attempts — you can book a new test immediately after receiving your results. Most candidates who prepare strategically between attempts see meaningful score improvements within one or two attempts. If your score has not improved after two attempts, a more structured preparation programme targeting your weakest skill is strongly recommended.
What is the minimum IELTS score for Australia student visa?
+
The IELTS minimum for an Australian student visa (subclass 500) varies depending on the level of study. For most university degrees, the requirement is an overall band of 5.5 to 6.0 with no individual section below 5.0. For some health professions and intensive English programs, the requirements are higher. Always check the specific Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirements for your application directly with the Department of Home Affairs or your education provider's CRICOS entry.
What IELTS score do I need for Canada Express Entry?
+
For Canada Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker Program), IELTS scores are converted into Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) scores. The minimum language requirement is CLB 7 in all four abilities, which roughly corresponds to an IELTS score of 6.0 in each section. However, scoring higher CLB levels (CLB 9+, requiring approximately IELTS 7.0 in each section) significantly increases your Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points and your chances of receiving an Invitation to Apply (ITA).
Is IELTS accepted for UK visa applications?
+
Yes. IELTS Academic and IELTS General Training are both accepted for UK visa applications, but specific visa routes have their own requirements. For student visas (Student Route), applicants need to meet the Secure English Language Test (SELT) requirement using IELTS for UKVI Academic. For Skilled Worker visas, IELTS for UKVI General Training is required. Regular IELTS taken at standard test centres may not always be accepted for visa purposes — always check the UKVI-approved test list before booking.
How long does it take to get IELTS results?
+
For paper-based IELTS, results are typically available 13 calendar days after the test date. For computer-delivered IELTS, results are usually available within 3–5 days. Results are released online via the candidate portal and hard-copy Test Report Forms are sent by post. Most organisations will accept a digital result while waiting for the physical TRF, but always confirm with your institution or visa authority first.
Can I take IELTS online from home?
+
IELTS Online — a home-based version of the test — is available in select markets. It uses AI-powered remote proctoring and is available for both Academic and General Training formats. However, IELTS Online is not accepted by all institutions and immigration authorities. Before booking an online test, confirm explicitly with your university and visa authority that they accept IELTS Online. If there is any doubt, a test centre sitting is the safer choice.
What is the difference between IELTS and TOEFL?
+
Both IELTS and TOEFL are internationally accepted English proficiency tests. The key differences are in format and accent: IELTS uses a face-to-face Speaking interview and British, Australian, American, and New Zealand accents in Listening. TOEFL is entirely computer-based, including an integrated Speaking component recorded by the candidate. IELTS is more widely accepted for immigration purposes globally; TOEFL is preferred by some American universities. Most institutions accept both, so choose the format that suits your strengths.
How do I register for IELTS?
+
You can register for IELTS through the official British Council, IDP, or Cambridge websites, or directly at an authorised test centre. Registration typically involves creating an online account, selecting your preferred test date and location, uploading identification documents, and paying the test fee. Test fees vary by country but are typically in the range of 14,000 to 18,000 Indian Rupees. It is recommended to book at least 4 to 6 weeks in advance for popular test dates.
How much does IELTS cost in India?
+
The IELTS test fee in India is approximately ₹16,250 (IDP) or ₹17,000 (British Council) as of 2025–26, though fees are subject to change. Computer-delivered IELTS and paper-based IELTS are priced similarly. Some test centres may charge slightly different amounts. Always check the current fee on the official IDP India or British Council India website before registering, as fees are updated periodically.
What documents do I need to bring to the IELTS test?
+
You must bring the original identification document that you used during registration — typically a valid passport. No photocopies or alternative IDs are accepted. You should also bring your confirmation email or test booking slip. Watches, mobile phones, and electronic devices must be left outside the test room. Water in a clear, unlabelled bottle is usually permitted. Always check the specific requirements from your test centre ahead of the test date.
Can I appeal my IELTS score?
+
Yes. If you believe your Writing or Speaking score does not reflect your actual ability, you can apply for an Enquiry on Results (EOR) within six weeks of your test date. There is a fee for the EOR, which is refunded if your score is upgraded. Listening and Reading are machine-marked but can also be re-checked for marking errors. The EOR process typically takes six to eight weeks, so factor this into your application timeline if your score is borderline for an important deadline.
Does IELTS have negative marking?
+
No. IELTS does not use negative marking in any section. In Listening and Reading, every correct answer gains one mark and incorrect or blank answers score zero. This means you should always write an answer for every question — even if you are not certain. Leaving an answer blank is always worse than making an educated guess, since you can never lose marks by answering.
What is the minimum IELTS score for New Zealand permanent residency?
+
For New Zealand's Skilled Migrant Category Residence Visa, the standard English language requirement is met by an IELTS overall band of 6.5 with no component below 6.0 (or equivalent in TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, or other approved tests). Some sub-categories and occupations have different requirements. Always verify the specific English language requirement for your visa subclass on the Immigration New Zealand website, as requirements are reviewed periodically.
How do I improve my IELTS Listening score?
+
To improve your Listening score, focus on three things: first, train your ear with varied English accents — BBC World Service, Australian Broadcasting Corporation, and NPR are excellent free resources. Second, always predict the answer type before each section by reading the questions carefully during the preparation time. Third, review every incorrect answer after each practice test and understand exactly why you missed it — guessing, distraction, unfamiliar vocabulary, or speed. Targeted review of errors is far more valuable than repeated practice without analysis.
What is the best way to prepare for IELTS Writing Task 2?
+
The most effective IELTS Writing Task 2 preparation combines three things: understanding the five essay question types (opinion, discussion, advantages/disadvantages, problem/solution, and two-part questions), practising a consistent essay structure that works for each type, and reviewing sample Band 7–9 responses to understand what examiner-approved writing actually looks like. Writing one full essay per week under timed conditions and getting it reviewed — whether by a teacher, a study partner, or a language model — is the fastest way to identify and correct persistent weaknesses.
Is IELTS required for German university admission?
+
For German-taught programs, applicants typically need to demonstrate German language proficiency (DSH, TestDaF, or Goethe-Zertifikat). For English-taught programs at German universities — which are increasingly common — IELTS Academic with a score of 6.0 to 7.0 is widely accepted. Requirements vary by university and program. Some German universities also accept TOEFL iBT as an alternative. Always check the specific requirements of your target institution, as German universities set their own English language criteria independently.
How can I check if a scholarship requires IELTS?
+
The simplest way is to use our IELTS Scholarship Eligibility Checker, which covers the IELTS requirements for major international scholarship programmes. Alternatively, each scholarship's official website lists its language requirements — usually under "Eligibility" or "How to Apply." For government scholarships like Chevening, Commonwealth, DAAD, and Fulbright, language requirements are also listed on the scholarship's official country-specific pages.
What happens if I score differently in different IELTS sections?
+
Uneven section scores are very common and perfectly normal. Your Overall Band Score is the average of all four, so a lower score in one section is partially offset by higher scores in others. However, many institutions and visa categories set minimum section scores — not just a minimum overall. For example, a university might require overall 7.0 with no section below 6.5. If your individual section scores do not meet these minimums, you will need to retake the exam even if your overall score is sufficient. Always check section minimums, not just the overall requirement.
