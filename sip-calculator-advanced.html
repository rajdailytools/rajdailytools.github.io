Complete Guide

SIP Complete Guide 2026 — Beginner to Advanced

SIP kya hai, Step-Up kya hota hai, LTCG tax, real-life examples, aur long-term wealth strategy — sab yahan.

What is SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals — typically monthly. When you set up a SIP, a fixed amount is automatically debited from your bank account every month and invested into your chosen mutual fund scheme at the current NAV (Net Asset Value).

SIP is the most popular and disciplined way to invest in mutual funds. It removes the need to time the market and works on the principle of rupee cost averaging — buying more units when markets are low and fewer when markets are high, automatically averaging out your purchase cost over time.

What is Step-Up SIP?

A Step-Up SIP (also called Top-Up SIP) allows you to automatically increase your SIP amount by a fixed percentage every year. For example, if you start with ₹5,000/month and choose a 10% annual step-up, your SIP becomes:

  • Year 1: ₹5,000/month
  • Year 2: ₹5,500/month
  • Year 3: ₹6,050/month
  • Year 5: ₹7,321/month
  • Year 10: ₹11,789/month
  • Year 20: ₹30,577/month
Step-Up SIP Power Example: ₹5,000/month flat SIP for 20 years at 12% = ₹49.96 lakh. Same ₹5,000 with 10% annual step-up for 20 years at 12% = ₹1.27 Cr — that's 2.5× more wealth by just increasing your SIP with your salary!

How SIP Returns are Calculated

SIP returns use the Future Value of Annuity formula: FV = P × [((1+r)^n – 1) / r] × (1+r), where P = monthly SIP amount, r = monthly return rate (annual rate ÷ 12), n = total months. For Step-Up SIP, each year's SIP amount is calculated separately and summed across years accounting for compound growth on each instalment.

LTCG Tax on Mutual Fund SIP (2024–25)

As per the Finance Act 2024, Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on equity mutual funds are taxed at 12.5% (changed from 10% in Budget 2024) on gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh per year (exemption limit unchanged at ₹1 lakh for FY25). Holding period for LTCG qualification is more than 12 months. Short-term gains (STCG) are taxed at 20%.

  • LTCG rate: 12.5% on equity mutual fund gains above ₹1.25 lakh/year
  • STCG rate: 20% for equity funds held less than 12 months
  • Debt mutual funds: Taxed as per income tax slab (no LTCG benefit)
  • ELSS funds: 3-year lock-in, LTCG applies after lock-in period

Real-Life SIP Examples — ₹1,000/month

See the incredible power of even a small ₹1,000 monthly SIP across different time horizons at 12% CAGR:

  • 5 years: Invested ₹60,000 → Corpus ₹81,670 → Gain: ₹21,670 (36%)
  • 10 years: Invested ₹1,20,000 → Corpus ₹2,32,339 → Gain: ₹1,12,339 (94%)
  • 15 years: Invested ₹1,80,000 → Corpus ₹5,02,575 → Gain: ₹3,22,575 (179%)
  • 20 years: Invested ₹2,40,000 → Corpus ₹9,99,148 → Gain: ₹7,59,148 (316%)
  • 30 years: Invested ₹3,60,000 → Corpus ₹35,29,464 → Gain: ₹31,69,464 (880%)

₹1,000/month for 30 years at 12% = nearly ₹35 lakh. That's the pure power of compounding and patience.

Inflation Warning: ₹35 lakh in 30 years at 6% inflation has a real purchasing power of only ₹6.1 lakh in today's money. This is why long-term investors should target 14–15% returns to significantly beat inflation, or increase SIP amounts aggressively over time.

Common SIP Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping SIP during market downturns — this locks in your losses and you miss the recovery upswing
  • Investing in too many funds — 3–5 well-chosen funds are better than 20 random ones
  • Choosing funds based only on last 1-year returns — always check 5 and 10-year rolling returns
  • Not reviewing portfolio once a year — rebalance if any asset class drifts more than 10% from target
  • Redeeming SIP for short-term needs — keep a separate emergency fund so SIP is never disturbed
  • Not accounting for LTCG tax in goal planning — your post-tax corpus is what you actually receive

Long-Term Wealth Strategy — The 3-Fund Approach

For most Indian retail investors, a simple 3-fund portfolio with monthly SIP works best: Nifty 50 index fund (50%), Nifty Next 50 / Midcap 150 index fund (30%), and a Debt/Short-Term fund (20%). Low expense ratio, diversified, and rebalanced annually. With a 10% step-up SIP on this portfolio, building ₹1 Cr+ corpus in 12–15 years on a ₹10,000/month SIP is very achievable.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

SIP investment ke baare mein sabse common sawaal.

What is SIP and how does it work?+
SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method where a fixed amount is automatically invested in a mutual fund every month. The fund manager allocates units based on the current NAV. Over time, you accumulate units and benefit from rupee cost averaging (buying more units when NAV is low, fewer when high) and compounding (earning returns on returns). It's the most disciplined and proven way to build wealth in India.
What is Step-Up SIP and why is it better?+
Step-Up SIP means increasing your monthly SIP amount by a fixed % every year (typically 10–15%). As your income grows, your investment grows too. It dramatically increases your final corpus — often 2–3× more than a flat SIP — because you're contributing more during middle and later years when compounding is at its strongest. Most AMCs offer automatic step-up SIP options. This calculator lets you model and visualize this exact scenario.
How accurate is this SIP calculator?+
This calculator uses industry-standard formulas: Future Value of Annuity for regular SIP, compounded future value with annual SIP escalation for Step-Up SIP, and reverse FV calculation for Goal Planner. Results are mathematically precise given the inputs. However, actual mutual fund returns depend on market performance and will differ from projections. Use conservative return estimates (10–12% for equity) for realistic planning.
What return rate should I use for SIP planning?+
Conservative estimate: 10–11% (suitable for 5–7 year planning). Moderate: 12% (long-term Nifty 50 historical CAGR). Optimistic: 14–15% (midcap/smallcap historical CAGR, but with higher volatility). Never use above 18% for serious planning — it leads to grossly unrealistic expectations. For debt fund SIP, use 6–7%. Remember: even Nifty 50 has had periods with near-zero returns over 5 years during bear markets.
What is LTCG tax and how does it affect my SIP?+
LTCG (Long Term Capital Gains) tax of 12.5% applies to equity mutual fund gains above ₹1.25 lakh per year (as per Finance Act 2024). For a large corpus built through SIP, this tax can significantly reduce your take-home returns. For example, if your SIP earns ₹20 lakh in gains, approximately ₹2.34 lakh may go toward LTCG tax. This calculator estimates your post-LTCG value — toggle it on in Advanced Options. Note: Each SIP instalment starts its own 12-month holding period clock.
Can I use the Goal Planner to calculate required SIP?+
Yes! The Goal Planner mode works in reverse — you enter your target corpus amount (e.g., ₹1 Cr for retirement), expected return rate, and time available. The calculator tells you exactly how much monthly SIP you need to reach that goal. This is called reverse SIP calculation and is extremely useful for goal-based financial planning (child's education, marriage, home down payment, retirement corpus).
Is SIP safe? What if markets crash?+
SIP in equity mutual funds is market-linked and not risk-free — your portfolio value will fall during market crashes. However, SIP's rupee cost averaging benefit actually works best during crashes — you accumulate more units at lower prices. Historically, every major market crash (2008, 2020, etc.) recovered within 2–4 years for Nifty 50. Investors who continued SIP through these crashes saw exceptional returns. The key is to stay invested for 7+ years and never stop SIP during downturns.
