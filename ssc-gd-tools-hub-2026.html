What is SSC GD Constable Recruitment?

SSC GD stands for Staff Selection Commission's General Duty recruitment exam. It is conducted by the Staff Selection Commission on behalf of the Ministry of Home Affairs to fill Constable (General Duty) posts in the Central Armed Police Forces — Border Security Force (BSF), Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP), Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB), Secretariat Security Force (SSF) — as well as Rifleman (General Duty) posts in Assam Rifles (AR). It is one of the largest uniformed-services recruitment drives in the country, with vacancies running into tens of thousands almost every cycle, which is exactly why lakhs of aspirants across every state prepare for it every year.

Unlike many state police exams that are limited to one region, SSC GD is genuinely pan-India. A candidate from Bhopal competes for a vacancy earmarked for Madhya Pradesh, a candidate from Guwahati competes for an Assam vacancy, and so on — while SSF vacancies alone are filled purely on an all-India merit basis. This state-wise and force-wise structure is exactly why so many candidates get confused about eligibility, posting, and merit — and exactly why a set of clear, purpose-built calculators makes the entire journey easier to understand.

How the Recruitment Process Works

The SSC GD selection journey happens in four broad stages, and every one of the 20 tools in this guide is mapped to one of these stages so you always know exactly where a tool fits into your preparation timeline.

  • Computer Based Examination (CBE): An objective-type paper of 80 questions (160 marks) covering Reasoning, General Knowledge, Elementary Maths and English/Hindi, completed in 60 minutes with negative marking.
  • Physical Efficiency Test (PET) & Physical Standard Test (PST): A qualifying-nature running test plus height, weight and chest measurement against prescribed standards.
  • Detailed Medical Examination (DME) & Review Medical Examination (RME): A structured medical check covering vision, hearing, dental health and physical structure.
  • Document Verification (DV): Verification of educational, age, domicile, category and other certificates, usually conducted alongside DME.

The final merit list and force allocation are based on your normalized CBE score (plus NCC bonus marks if applicable) combined with your force preference order — subject to qualifying every other stage. Because so many separate numbers, standards and formulas are involved across these four stages, we've built one calculator for each specific question an aspirant typically has.

This guide uses only evergreen SSC GD rules — eligibility, physical standards, exam pattern, salary structure and selection process — sourced from the official Notice of Examination. Any date-specific information (application dates, exam schedule, admit card release) changes every cycle, so please always confirm those from ssc.gov.in directly.

Why These 20 Tools Help You Prepare Smarter

Most SSC GD aspirants make the same mistakes — not knowing they're a few months over the age limit until it's too late, misjudging their height or running time against the wrong regional standard, or having no real sense of what their raw exam score actually converts to once negative marking and normalization are applied. Each tool below is built to answer exactly one such question in under two minutes, using only the official evergreen standards, so you can plan with clarity instead of guesswork.

SSC GD Age Eligibility Calculator

Enter your date of birth and category to instantly check whether you fall within the SSC GD age bracket, including all applicable relaxations.

Age eligibility is the very first filter in SSC GD recruitment, and it is also the filter with zero flexibility — there is no correction window, no appeal, and no exception for a candidate who is even one day outside the permitted range. Every year, some genuinely capable candidates lose their chance simply because they miscalculated their age against the notified cut-off date, or forgot to apply their category's relaxation correctly. This calculator removes that risk entirely.

What Does This Tool Check?

  • Your exact age as of the notified cut-off date
  • Whether you fall within the base 18–23 year bracket
  • Your applicable age relaxation based on category
  • Your final eligible age window after relaxation
Official rule: The base age bracket is 18 to 23 years as on the cut-off date fixed in the year's notice. Relaxation beyond the upper limit is 5 years for SC/ST, 3 years for OBC, and 3 years for Ex-Servicemen after deducting their military service from actual age. Additional relaxation of 5, 8, or 10 years applies to children and dependents of victims killed in the 1984 riots, depending on category. Date of Birth is finalised strictly as per the Matriculation certificate.

SSC GD Cut Off Predictor

Check your expected raw score against the official minimum qualifying percentage for your category, and understand where you realistically stand.

"What is the SSC GD cut off?" is probably the single most searched question by aspirants every year — and it's also the most misunderstood one. There is no single universal cut off number; there is only a minimum qualifying percentage fixed by category, applied to normalized marks, which then interacts with the number of vacancies and the strength of that year's competition to produce the actual shortlisting line. This tool focuses on the one part of the equation that is fixed and knowable in advance — the official minimum qualifying percentage — and gives you an honest, non-inflated read on your raw score.

What Does This Tool Check?

  • Your raw score's percentage out of the maximum 160 marks
  • Whether that percentage clears your category's minimum qualifying mark
  • How much buffer (or shortfall) you have above the minimum
Official rule: Minimum qualifying marks in the CBE (without NCC bonus) are 30% for Unreserved candidates, 25% for OBC/EWS candidates, and 20% for all other categories including SC, ST, and Ex-Servicemen. These are the floor requirements to even be considered for PET/PST shortlisting — actual shortlisting is then done strictly by merit within about 8 times the vacancies.
CategoryMinimum Qualifying %Marks (out of 160)
Unreserved (UR)30%48 marks
OBC / EWS25%40 marks
SC / ST / ESM / Others20%32 marks

📊 Check Your Score Against the Minimum Line

This tool checks only the fixed minimum qualifying line, not the dynamic shortlisting line, which depends on that year's competition and vacancies. Use it as a floor check, not a final prediction.

Detailed Explanation

In practice, simply crossing the minimum qualifying percentage rarely guarantees a PET/PST call, because shortlisting is done purely on merit within roughly eight times the number of vacancies for that state/category combination. A candidate scoring 90 out of 160 may comfortably clear the UR minimum of 48, yet still miss the actual shortlisting line in a high-competition state. That's exactly why serious aspirants generally aim well above the minimum — often 55–65% or higher in a good attempt year — even though no official number is published in advance.

ExampleAn OBC candidate scores 72 out of 160. Their percentage is 45%, comfortably above the 25% OBC minimum. This confirms they clear the qualifying floor — but their actual shortlisting chances still depend on how competitive their specific state and category turn out to be that year.
Important: The minimum qualifying percentage is checked on your normalized score, not your raw score — normalization can shift your effective score slightly, so treat this tool as an estimate.
Common mistake: Assuming that clearing the minimum qualifying percentage means guaranteed selection to the next round — it only means eligibility to be considered in the merit-based shortlist.

SSC GD Attempt Eligibility Tool

SSC GD does not cap the number of attempts directly — your age bracket does. This tool estimates roughly how many future recruitment cycles you can realistically apply in.

A lot of aspirants assume every government exam has a fixed "number of attempts" rule like some civil service exams do. SSC GD does not work that way — there is no attempt limit mentioned anywhere in the Notice of Examination. What actually limits you is simply your age bracket. As long as your age falls within the permitted range (with relaxation, if applicable) for that year's notice, you can apply, regardless of how many times you've applied before.

Official rule: The Notice of Examination sets an age bracket, not an attempt count. There is no clause anywhere restricting how many times a candidate may apply, provided they remain within the eligible age window (including any applicable relaxation) for that specific year's recruitment cycle.

🎖️ Understand Your Preference Logic

This tool explains allocation logic; it cannot predict which force you'll actually get, since that depends on that year's vacancies and every other candidate's merit and preferences too.

Detailed Explanation

Force allocation works like a merit-based seat-matching process: candidates are considered in strict order of merit, and each candidate is offered their highest-ranked preference among forces that still have vacancies available for their category, state, and gender at that point. If your top preference is already filled by higher-merit candidates by the time your rank comes up, the system moves to your second preference, and so on. This is exactly why filling out genuine preferences — rather than copying someone else's order — actually matters: a candidate who ranks a less crowded force higher may get allocated there faster than one who stacks only the most popular forces at the top.

ExampleA candidate ranks CISF first because of its larger vacancy count, followed by BSF and CRPF. Since CISF alone accounts for the largest share of GD vacancies most cycles, ranking it highly realistically improves the odds of getting a preferred allocation compared to stacking only smaller-vacancy forces at the top.
Common mistake: Ranking forces purely by "reputation" without considering vacancy volume — a force with far fewer seats will always be harder to get regardless of how high you rank it.

Frequently Asked Questions

20 common SSC GD questions answered clearly and honestly.

What is the age limit for SSC GD Constable?+
Candidates generally need to be between 18 and 23 years old as on the cut-off date fixed for that year's notice, with relaxation of 5 years for SC/ST, 3 years for OBC, and additional relaxation for Ex-Servicemen and certain other categories as specified in the notice.
What is the minimum height required for SSC GD Constable?+
The general standard is 170cm for male candidates and 157cm for female candidates, with relaxed height standards for Scheduled Tribe candidates, hill-state candidates, and candidates from several North Eastern states and specific regions.
What is the SSC GD exam pattern?+
The Computer Based Examination has 80 objective questions worth 2 marks each (160 total marks), divided equally across General Intelligence & Reasoning, General Knowledge & Awareness, Elementary Mathematics, and English/Hindi, to be completed in 60 minutes with 0.25 negative marking per wrong answer.
What is the SSC GD selection process?+
Selection involves a Computer Based Examination, followed by Physical Efficiency Test and Physical Standard Test, then Detailed Medical Examination and Document Verification, with the final merit list based on normalized CBE marks plus NCC bonus marks if applicable.
What is the SSC GD running test requirement?+
Male candidates must cover 5 km within 24 minutes, and female candidates must cover 1.6 km within 8.5 minutes. Candidates from the Ladakh region have separate standards of 1.6 km in 7 minutes for males and 800 metres in 5 minutes for females.
What is the SSC GD Constable salary?+
SSC GD Constable posts carry Pay Level-3 of the 7th CPC Pay Matrix, with a basic pay ranging from ₹21,700 to ₹69,100, in addition to applicable allowances such as DA, HRA, and other force-specific benefits.
Is there negative marking in SSC GD exam?+
Yes, 0.25 marks are deducted for every wrong answer in the Computer Based Examination, so candidates are advised to attempt questions carefully rather than guessing randomly.
What are the minimum qualifying marks in SSC GD CBE?+
Unreserved category candidates generally need 30% marks, OBC/EWS candidates need 25%, and all other categories including SC, ST, and ESM need 20% marks in the Computer Based Examination to be considered for the next stage.
How is chest measured for SSC GD?+
Chest measurement in SSC GD is not separately notified with an expansion figure the way some other physical tests are; female candidates are not chest-measured at all, though it is confirmed that the chest is well-developed.
Do women get any relaxation in SSC GD physical standards?+
Yes, women have a separate height standard of 157cm against 170cm for men, a shorter running distance and time in the PET, and special provisions including a pregnancy declaration process before the Physical Efficiency Test.
What is NCC bonus marks in SSC GD?+
NCC 'C' Certificate holders get 5% bonus of the maximum exam marks, 'B' Certificate holders get 3%, and 'A' Certificate holders get 2%, provisionally added to the normalized score, subject to certificate verification at Document Verification.
Is normalization used in SSC GD result calculation?+
Yes, because the exam is conducted across multiple shifts and days, SSC applies a normalization procedure to make scores comparable across sessions before preparing the final merit list.
How many candidates are shortlisted for PET/PST in SSC GD?+
Based on merit in the Computer Based Examination, roughly 8 times the number of vacancies are shortlisted for the Physical Efficiency Test and Physical Standard Test stage, with a higher ratio typically applied for SSF vacancies.
How many candidates are shortlisted for Document Verification in SSC GD?+
Candidates who qualify PET/PST are further shortlisted for Detailed Medical Examination and Document Verification at roughly 2 times the number of vacancies, based on their CBE merit.
Can I choose my preferred force in SSC GD?+
Yes, candidates must rank all seven force options (BSF, CISF, CRPF, SSB, ITBP, Assam Rifles, SSF) in order of priority, and final force allocation is based on merit-cum-preference, so higher merit combined with a well-thought-out preference order improves the chance of getting a preferred force.
Are SSC GD vacancies state-specific?+
Yes, except for SSF which is filled on an all-India basis, vacancies in other CAPFs are earmarked state/UT-wise based on the domicile state and district a candidate declares, and some vacancies are further reserved for border and Naxal/militancy-affected districts.
What documents are required at SSC GD Document Verification?+
Commonly required documents include the Matriculation certificate for age and qualification proof, domicile/PRC certificate, caste certificate where applicable, NCC certificate if claimed, Ex-Servicemen documents if applicable, and any certificate supporting relaxation claims.
Is there a fixed number of attempts allowed in SSC GD?+
SSC GD does not impose a separate "number of attempts" cap the way some other exams do; eligibility is governed purely by the age bracket for that year's notice, so a candidate can apply in every cycle they fall within the permitted age range.
What medical conditions can disqualify a candidate in SSC GD?+
Medical examination follows uniform CAPF/Assam Rifles medical guidelines covering vision, hearing, dental health, and structural conditions such as flat foot or knock knee; significant issues in these areas can lead to a candidate being declared medically unfit.
Do SSC GD candidates get a waiting list?+
No, the Commission does not prepare or maintain a waiting or reserve list after the declaration of the final result of the examination.
